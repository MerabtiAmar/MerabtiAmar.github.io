Eight experiments

Each project starts from a question. I write down what I did, what I measured, and what did not work: that is often where I learned the most.

  1. Exp. 01
    2026
    personal project

    Can you train a language model for a language with almost no data?

    All available written Kabyle amounts to about 40 million tokens, partly locked in PDFs typeset with pre-Unicode fonts. I wrote the decoder for those fonts, built a 120-million-character corpus, then pretrained a Llama-style Transformer before fine-tuning it on chat messages.

    What did not workMy first model (43M parameters for 1M tokens) memorized its corpus: training loss 1.33, validation loss 6.21. It was about 800 times too large for its data.

  2. Exp. 02
    2026
    Nricher internship
    MSc thesis, Prof. Salima Benbernou

    How do you verify a value extracted from a product page when the client's catalog is unreliable?

    Each attribute/value pair becomes a hypothesis, checked sentence by sentence against the product description with textual inference (mDeBERTa-v3). These scores, together with BGE-M3 embeddings, feed TabPFN. The benchmark covers 4 production catalogs: 35 attributes, 30,919 rows and 5,635 paired runs, with a reference built without manual annotation.

    What the ablation showedRemoving the 4 inference scores costs 8.9 points; removing the 50 embedding components costs only 1.9 (Wilcoxon test). What matters is how the text representation is oriented, not how rich it is.

    Read the experiment →code owned by Nricher, not published
    68%
    of values validated automatically, at 99% precision
    0.925
    balanced accuracy, versus 0.808 for the inference rule alone and 0.898 for TabSTAR, 28 times more expensive
  3. Exp. 03
    2026
    personal projects

    What can agents learn when you give them no rules at all?

    I wrote a 2D football engine in NumPy (FIFA proportions, possession, tackles, collisions) and trained a single PPO policy that plays both sides: two players spawn anywhere on the pitch and try to score, with no game rule coded inside the agent. 36 million steps in 48 minutes, on a laptop without a GPU. In parallel, a quadruped simulated with pymunk learns to walk to a flag with Stable-Baselines3.

    What did not workPotential-based shaping, the safest form in theory, gives no signal when every match ends: 1% wins. An AlphaStar-style opponent pool loses to mirror self-play. And my first evaluations, on 12 matches from a fixed kick-off, were in fact measuring a single repeated match. The quadruped, for its part, never reaches its target among obstacles (0/20).

    Read the experiment →Football ↗Quadruped ↗
    73%
    wins against a scripted player, over 500 matches, against 19% losses
    Quadruped: 20/20 targets reached on flat ground, 16/20 on uneven terrain
  4. Exp. 04
    2025
    MSc year 1, DCI
    supervised by Prof. Themis Palpanas

    To fill gaps in a time series, does deep learning beat a classical method?

    Electricity meters from 3,127 households, 12,864 readings each, 30 to 50% of values masked. We compared a nearest neighbor, an LSTM autoencoder, a partial-convolution U-Net, a self-supervised Transformer and an STL decomposition rewritten in PyTorch to run on GPU.

    AnswerNot here. STL wins in both scenarios, including on 1,098 unseen households: trend and seasonality are exactly the structure of this data, and the deep models had to be scaled down to fit on 4 GPUs.

    Read the experiment →Code and report ↗
    0.262
    MAE for GPU STL, versus 0.327 for the best deep model (U-Net)
  5. Exp. 05
    2025
    MSc research project

    How good are three 7-billion-parameter open-source LLMs at maths, common sense and code?

    LLaMA 2, Mistral and distilled DeepSeek-R1, served with Ollama on 4 L4 GPUs: GSM8K 8-shot, HellaSwag and HumanEval 0-shot, with generated code executed in a sandbox and Python error types analyzed.

    NuanceDeepSeek-R1 dominates coding, but its long reasoning makes it about 100 times slower, and it gave a usable answer to only 258 maths questions out of 500. Mistral offers the best trade-off between accuracy and latency.

    Read the experiment →Code and report ↗
    128/164
    HumanEval problems solved by DeepSeek-R1 (78.0%), versus 52 for Mistral and 15 for LLaMA 2
  6. Exp. 06
    2023–2024
    ENAGEO, Sonatrach group
    internship, then fixed-term contract

    Can you reconstruct a well measurement where it is missing, and predict it in another well?

    Along a borehole, rock properties such as elasticity or density are measured at regular depth intervals. These series have gaps (faulty equipment, lost data) and the measurements are expensive. As an intern, I tested classical models from one well to another and delivered a Java Swing application to geophysicists. On the fixed-term contract, I treated these series as time series, with LSTMs and RNNs: first to fill the gaps within a well, then to predict the same property in another well.

    What did not workDuring the internship, linear regression (r = 0.15) and SVM (0.29) did not generalize from one well to another. And even at 0.82 with LSTMs, the predictions were not yet good enough for the company to use: I left the project in development when I moved to France.

    Read the experiment →Internship application (synthetic data) ↗
    0.52 → 0.82
    cross-well correlation, from internship (MLP) to contract (LSTM), with MAE down from 21.4 to 15.8
    12.7
    MAE when filling the gaps of a series within the same well (RMSE 15.2)
  7. Exp. 07
    2026
    personal project

    When a human can only annotate two hundred examples, which ones should they get?

    Five selection strategies compared at a fixed budget, on two datasets and two classifiers: twenty independent draws per strategy, paired comparisons and a Wilcoxon test. The sandbox on the page puts you in the annotator's seat.

    NuanceEntropy, the most taught strategy, does worse than random sampling at the start: 0.687 against 0.799 at fifty labels. And on an easy binary problem every strategy converges — active learning only pays off on hard tasks.

    Read the experiment →Code and results ↗
    99 %
    of the final accuracy reached with 16% of the labels, against 96% for random sampling of the same size
  8. Exp. 08
    2026
    personal project

    Can you open a Transformer and show, head by head, how it gets there?

    A 105,000-parameter model — two layers, two heads — trained in 2.3 minutes on a laptop to find in its context what it should write next, then dissected: each head ablated, its attention signature measured, its contribution quantified. The model runs in the page, with a switch per head.

    What did not workMy first version of the task kept a fixed motif length: a single-layer model solved it at 100% by plain offset, and my negative control controlled nothing. With a random period it falls back to 40%.

§ 4

What I'm looking for

From October 2026, two paths interest me equally.

Path 1

A CIFRE PhD

A CIFRE PhD is a three-year doctorate carried out jointly by a company and an academic lab in France. I am looking for a topic where the data itself is the problem: scarce, noisy, incomplete or unreliable. Language models and NLP, tabular, medical or biological data, physics, chemistry: the field matters less to me than the scientific question and its value for the company.

  • A first taste of research in industry: my MSc thesis at Nricher worked on 4 production catalogs.
  • Rigorous protocols: references without manual annotation, ablations, paired tests, calibration.
  • The habit of implementing things myself, from a Transformer to a simulation engine.
Path 2

A permanent data scientist role

I want to join a team that builds models people actually use, and that measures what they are worth before deploying them.

  • End-to-end pipelines: ingestion with Kafka and Spark, modeling, evaluation, application.
  • Two industry experiences with measured results: ENAGEO (0.52 → 0.82) and Nricher (68% automated at 99% precision).
  • Hands-on experience with LLMs, from fine-tuning to benchmarking.
